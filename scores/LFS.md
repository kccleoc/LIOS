# LFS — LIOS Fit Score

Answers: "How much does this investment improve the current LIOS portfolio?"

## LFS Constitution (Rules 1–5)

1. **LOS is absolute.** It evaluates the investment itself.
2. **LFS is relative.** It evaluates the investment against the current portfolio.
3. **LFS cannot rescue a poor investment.** LOS remains the primary investment gate.
4. **LFS is dynamic.** It changes whenever portfolio composition changes.
5. **Preference rule:** When LOS values are similar (within 5 points), LIOS should prefer the investment with the higher LFS.

## Five LFS Components

| # | Dimension | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Diversification Value** | Does the company introduce a new economic engine? |
| 2 | **Strategic Fit** | Does this strengthen the long-term LIOS mission? |
| 3 | **Overlap** | How much exposure already exists? |
| 4 | **Conviction Compatibility** | Can LIOS understand and monitor this business? |
| 5 | **Portfolio Balance** | Does this improve overall portfolio construction? |

## LFS Scale

| LFS Range | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **90–100** | **Exceptional** | Adds meaningful diversification without sacrificing expected returns |
| **80–89** | **Strong Fit** | Improves balance while maintaining high quality |
| **70–79** | **Useful** | Adds some diversification but is not essential |
| **60–69** | **Neutral** | Does not materially improve the portfolio |
| **Below 60** | **Poor Fit** | Adds unnecessary overlap or weak strategic value |

## Investment Decision Matrix

| LOS | LFS | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | **Highest priority for new capital** |
| High | Low | Excellent company, but lower portfolio priority |
| Low | High | Do not buy. Diversification alone is insufficient. |
| Low | Low | Reject |

## Economic Engine Principle

Diversification is measured by underlying economic drivers, not sector labels. Adding another company driven by the same economic engine should receive a lower LFS than adding a high-quality company driven by a new economic engine.

Economic engines include: AI Compute, AI Memory, AI Platforms, Industrial Automation, Consumer Spending, Digital Payments, Healthcare Innovation, Defense, Energy Infrastructure, Enterprise Software, Logistics, Utilities.
