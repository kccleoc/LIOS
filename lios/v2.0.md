## FIVE CAPITAL LAYERS

1. **Sleeve A — Core Compounding** (permanent targets; form Locked per L2)
   See `state.yaml: sleeve_a.allocation` for ETF tickers + within-Sleeve weights.
   Total size target: `state.yaml: sleeve_a.target_base_pct` (~70-75% of Core Portfolio (NLV − D − SR) by default).
   When SR = $0 this simplifies to ~70-75% of (NLV − D).
   `state.yaml: sleeve_a.permanently_exited` — portfolio-local list of
   tickers irrevocably excluded (formerly L7; re-entry is a rule violation).
   The cash-like yield buffer is cash, NOT a duration hedge.

2. **Sleeve B — Proven Alpha**
   Tickers live in `state.yaml: positions` (filtered to sleeve: B).
   Caps live in `state.yaml: sleeve_b_caps` (per `state.yaml` ticker+cap_pct pairs).
   Caps are BOTH buying-pause AND trim triggers (form Locked per L3).
   Names assigned to Sleeve D stay in D, not B, even if conviction is high.

3. **Sleeve C — Tactical Cash**
   Target `state.yaml: sleeve_c.target_pct_of_abc` (~5% of A+B+C base by default)
   Holds: Kelly Reserve + short-term tactical deployments

4. **Sleeve D — Frontier Innovation**
   Target `state.yaml: sleeve_d.target_pct_of_total_nlv` (5% of TOTAL NLV by default)
   Funded ONLY from injected/external capital —
   NEVER from DCA, Sleeve A/B sales, or Kelly Reserve
   Current holdings: see `state.yaml: positions` (filtered to sleeve: D)
   Each D position carries a `stage` field (1=Watchlist, 2=Pilot, 3=Full position).

5. **Strategic Reserve (SR)** — NOT a sleeve
   Undeployed capital awaiting superior expected returns.
   Funded ONLY by: new external capital, exceptional profit
   realization, windfalls.
   NEVER funded by: DCA, Sleeve A sales, Sleeve B trimming,
   Sleeve D sales, Kelly Reserve.
   Default injection split: 30% deploy immediately / 70% SR
   (Investment Committee may modify).
   Deploys only on trigger: S&P correction >8-10%, Nasdaq
   correction >10%, high-conviction unjustified selloff,
   earnings confirm stronger thesis while price declines,
   Kelly Factor increases, or IC formal approval.
   Deployment hierarchy: 1) existing high-conviction Sleeve B
   2) Sleeve D expansion (must still satisfy D staging rules —
   SR doesn't bypass Stage 2/3 gates) 3) new approved tech waves
   4) Sleeve A on broad valuation opportunity.
   Only the Investment Committee may approve full/partial SR
   deployment or recommend holding.

## SLEEVE D — REPLACEMENT INDEX (governs all D surgery)

Three-metric weighted score, each 0-100:
Frontier Score 40% / Thesis Momentum 35% / Diversification Value 25%

Replacement approved ONLY when BOTH:
(1) Candidate Replacement Index > Incumbent Replacement Index
(2) At least TWO of these four conditions:
    • Incumbent misses earnings or issues weak guidance
    • Incumbent thesis weakens materially
    • Candidate trades at substantially more attractive valuation
    • Candidate wave becomes significantly higher conviction

No replacement on short-term price movement, unrealized loss,
or excitement about a new name alone.

Scores are dynamic — re-reviewed after quarterly earnings,
guidance changes, material price moves, tech breakthroughs,
or competitive shifts. A score is an assessment, not a
permanent label.

## RESEARCH INTELLIGENCE LAYER (v1.4 (preserved for backward compat) — unchanged in v2.0)

Every security researched under LIOS v1.4 (preserved for backward compat)+ receives five scores,
producing a single overall measure of investment attractiveness.

### The Five Scores

| Code | Score | Measures | Typical inputs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LVS** | LIOS Value Score | Valuation attractiveness | Forward P/E, PEG, EV/EBITDA, FCF Yield, historical valuation, margin of safety |
| **LGS** | LIOS Growth Score | Long-term compounding potential | Revenue growth, EPS growth, cash flow growth, estimate revisions, industry growth, TAM |
| **LQS** | LIOS Quality Score | Business excellence | ROIC, ROE, gross margin, operating margin, FCF, balance sheet, capital allocation, moat |
| **LMS** | LIOS Momentum Score | Market confirmation | Relative strength, trend, earnings surprises, estimate revisions, institutional accumulation, technical strength |
| **LOS** | LIOS Overall Score | Composite attractiveness | Weighted blend of LVS/LGS/LQS/LMS with professional judgment |

All four component scores are 0-100. Higher = better on that dimension.

### LOS Derivation (default weighting — may evolve)

LOS is a professional-judgment composite. **Quality generally
carries the greatest influence**, followed by Growth, Value, then
Momentum. The exact weighting may evolve as LIOS improves, but
every LOS must be explainable.

Default starting weights:

| Component | Default Weight |
|---|---|
| LQS | 35% |
| LGS | 25% |
| LVS | 25% |
| LMS | 15% |

A weighting deviation from these defaults **must be disclosed**
in the score breakdown with one-line rationale.

### LOS Interpretation

| LOS Band | Label | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **90–100** | **Elite** | Highest conviction. Core compounder. Aggressively accumulate during normal pullbacks. Rarely sell on valuation alone. |
| **80–89** | **Buy** | Accumulate. Suitable for regular DCA. Eligible for Sleeve A or Sleeve B. |
| **70–79** | **Watch** | Build watchlist. Buy on meaningful correction. Starter position acceptable. |
| **60–69** | **Hold** | Existing holders monitor. New purchases require a strong catalyst. |
| **Below 60** | **Pass** | Avoid. Candidate for replacement if currently owned. |

### LOS — Kelly — Sleeves Integration

- **LOS decides WHICH assets deserve investment.**
- **Kelly decides HOW MUCH capital to deploy.**
- **Sleeves decide WHERE the capital lives (A/B/C/D/SR).**

LOS does **not** replace Kelly, the Replacement Index, or any
sleeve rule. It is the upstream research filter that ensures only
vetted assets enter Kelly-sizing decisions.

### Sleeve D — LOS + Frontier Score (complementary)

Sleeve D surgery still requires the **Replacement Index** (Frontier
40% / Momentum 35% / Diversification 25%). **LOS is an additional
evaluation layer**:

- **Frontier Score** identifies innovation potential (theme fit)
- **LOS** identifies overall investment quality (business + valuation)

Both should be considered before replacement decisions. A candidate
with superior Frontier Score but weak LOS (e.g., LOS <70) should
NOT replace an incumbent without explicit IC rationale.

## PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION INTELLIGENCE (v2.0 — new)

LIOS v2.0 introduces **LFS — LIOS Fit Score**, a metric that
answers one question:

> **"How much does this investment improve the current LIOS portfolio?"**

Unlike LOS, LFS is **not** a company quality score. It is
**portfolio-relative**. The same company may receive different LFS
values as the portfolio evolves.

### Relationship: LOS vs LFS

| Framework | Question | Nature |
|---|---|---|
| **LOS** | Is this an outstanding investment? | Absolute — evaluates the investment itself |
| **LFS** | Does owning this improve the portfolio? | Relative — evaluates against current portfolio |

Both are required. Neither replaces the other.

### LFS Constitution (Rules 1–5)

1. **LOS is absolute.** It evaluates the investment itself.
2. **LFS is relative.** It evaluates the investment against the current portfolio.
3. **LFS cannot rescue a poor investment.** A weak LOS cannot become a Buy simply because it diversifies the portfolio. LOS remains the primary investment gate.
4. **LFS is dynamic.** It changes whenever portfolio composition changes.
5. **Preference rule:** When LOS values are similar, LIOS should prefer the investment with the higher LFS. This is now an official decision rule.

### The Five LFS Components

| # | Dimension | Question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Diversification Value** | Does the company introduce a new economic engine? |
| 2 | **Strategic Fit** | Does this strengthen the long-term LIOS mission? |
| 3 | **Overlap** | How much exposure already exists? |
| 4 | **Conviction Compatibility** | Can LIOS understand and monitor this business? |
| 5 | **Portfolio Balance** | Does this improve overall portfolio construction? |

### Economic Engine Principle

Diversification is **not** measured by sector labels alone. LFS
evaluates diversification by **underlying economic drivers**.

Economic engines include: AI Compute, AI Memory, AI Platforms,
Industrial Automation, Consumer Spending, Digital Payments,
Healthcare Innovation, Defense, Energy Infrastructure, Enterprise
Software, Logistics, Utilities.

Adding another company driven by the **same** economic engine
should receive a **lower** LFS than adding a high-quality company
driven by a **new** economic engine.

### LFS Scale

| LFS Range | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **90–100** | **Exceptional** | Adds meaningful diversification without sacrificing expected returns |
| **80–89** | **Strong Fit** | Improves balance while maintaining high quality |
| **70–79** | **Useful** | Adds some diversification but is not essential |
| **60–69** | **Neutral** | Does not materially improve the portfolio |
| **Below 60** | **Poor Fit** | Adds unnecessary overlap or weak strategic value |

### Investment Decision Matrix

LOS and LFS must be evaluated together:

| LOS | LFS | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| High | High | **Highest priority for new capital** |
| High | Low | Excellent company, but lower portfolio priority |
| Low | High | Do not buy. Diversification alone is insufficient. |
| Low | Low | Reject |

### LFS Worked Example — TXN

**Today:** TXN — LOS 84 (Buy), LFS 92 (Exceptional)
**After TXN becomes a large position:** LOS 84 (Buy), LFS 78 (Useful)

Company unchanged. Portfolio changed. LFS reflects evolving portfolio fit.

### Three Classes of Knowledge

**Class 1 — Permanent** (rarely changes): constitution/, core/
**Class 2 — Operational** (changes weekly): portfolio/current/, workspace/
**Class 3 — Research** (changes continuously): research/, scores/

1. **Agent reads** constitution/ and core/ to load the framework
2. **Agent reads** portfolio/current/ for live state
3. **Agent consults** scores/ for quick reference
4. **Agent follows** playbooks/ for workflow procedures
5. **Agent writes** to workspace/ for temporary notes
6. **Agent proposes** changes via rfcs/
