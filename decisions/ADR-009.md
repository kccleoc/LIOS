# ADR-009 — LIOS v7.0.0: fresh redesign, no score, windowed mandates

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-11-18
**Deciders:** Owner (Neo), Muse (advisor)
**Supersedes:** ADR-008 (v6.0.0 vehicle)

## Context

On 2026-11-18 the owner asked for a full hostile review of LIOS v6.0.0:
count the Portfolio Rules exactly, find loopholes and unreasonable rules
(especially any that let irrational human operation override the system),
and assess the AI agent's role and guardrails. The review returned
19 findings, one of them severity S1: the governance document (marked
Constitutional) granted the Investment Committee an override the
constitution forbids, with discretionary events up to 3% of NLV per
quarter and no precedence rule. Other findings: the exit gate was
self-scored with free re-weighting; an accepted breach had no expiry;
the transition mandate had no frequency limit; there was no scope rule,
so external holdings were invisible to the caps; "the agent never
trades" was a convention, not a rule; a superseded version's ruling
still stood in derived documents; and the dust rule would have sold
positions at the next Rebalance.

The owner decided to upgrade to v7 as a **fresh redesign, not a patch**:
keep what works in v6, simplify where there are too many rules, no
contradictions. The v7 draft was then red-teamed by three independent
reviewers (an internal red team and two external model runs), and the
findings were fixed before adoption.

## Decision

LIOS v7.0.0 is adopted as a fresh redesign of the framework:

1. **Eight Portfolio Rules, P1′–P8′, plus subordinate P3a, are the
   constitution.** One amendment table, in Part I, is the only amendment
   table. A P-rule amendment takes effect 30 days after its ADR is
   recorded. There is no second table anywhere.
2. **No score.** There is no score in V7. The
   Regime Index is a dashboard only: it sizes nothing, shifts no target,
   gates no operation, and is not evidence for a thesis break or a
   replacement decision. Sleeve B exits only on a thesis break,
   established by documented research and verification, decided at the
   quarterly review. Price and drawdown are never exit triggers.
3. **Mandate windows.** A transition mandate may be raised only inside
   one of three windows per year, bounded by 31 January, 31 July and
   31 October; at most one mandate per window, at most three raised in
   any actual calendar year, at least 90 days between raisings, sunset at
   most 92 days after raising. A mandate has eight requirements:
   sunset, completion criteria, fallback, cap compliance,
   window/window-year/actual-calendar-year cap, no overlap, minimum
   interval, and what a mandate may not do.
4. **True figures for caps.** The US-situs ceiling is tested on the true
   figure — the book plus listed external holdings — not the book figure
   alone. The scope record lists external securities and the operating
   cash held outside the book, so no holding is invisible to a cap.
5. **Venue-aware execution.** Execution windows are per listing venue:
   LSE-listed lines 10:00–11:30 ET, US-listed lines 10:00–15:30 ET.
   Limit orders only, within the band.
6. **Thirty validation checks.** Twelve data checks (abort on failure)
   and eighteen constitutional checks (report, never abort), including a
   trade ledger that tags every sale with its direction and operation.
   The checks are the controls; the Control map in Part VI records what
   each control is, whether it is defined, implemented and tested, and
   what happens on failure.
7. **The agent never trades — as a rule.** Part VII makes the agent's
   role constitutional: advisor to the owner, never an executor. The
   agent never places, modifies, or cancels an order, never commits or
   pushes without per-request approval, and never presents a draft
   framework as settled rules. The repo AI agent no longer handles LIOS;
   Muse implements directly after owner approval.
8. **Grandfathering is explicit.** Where the adopted book exceeds a v7
   limit on day one, the position is recorded as a grandfathered soft
   breach with a re-decision date — never a forced or comparative sale.

## Consequences

- Every v6 mechanism that produced a score is deleted: the scoring
  layers, the replacement ranking, the old section-11 scoreboard, and the
  commands that invoked them. Their definitions live only in git
  history and the frozen archives.
- The framework is leaner: fewer files, fewer commands, one amendment
  table, one set of checks. A file that no longer has a rule behind it
  is deleted, not marked retired.
- The public changelog for this release contains no live portfolio
  figures and no tickers. Figures live in the SSOT; the changelog
  describes rules.
- Adoption needs no trade: v7 is a rulebook change, and the portfolio
  already fits it, except for the recorded adoption position on the true
  US-situs figure, which is cured by a named December action.
- The next hostile review runs against the implemented files before
  tagging. Tagging is a separate step, not part of this ADR.
