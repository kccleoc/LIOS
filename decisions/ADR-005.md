# ADR-005 — Adopt LIOS v3.0 (Five Sleeves, Gap Deployment, Bull–Bear Index)

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Supersedes:** none (ADR-003 established the OSS bifurcation; ADR-005 changes the
methodology inside it)
**Proposal:** RFC-003

---

## Context

The v2.x methodology had accumulated four structural defects: a "Kelly Factor"
that was not the Kelly criterion and sized a flow rather than wealth; two
denominators (`ABC` for A and C, NLV for B caps) that made one dollar carry two
weights; a deployment score that ignored the distance to target; and no
duration-bearing asset anywhere in the architecture.

Each defect was individually defensible and collectively incoherent. The
deployment rule could not close the gap it was supposed to close, the hedgeless
cash sat in 0–1 year instruments that return approximately zero in an equity
selloff, and the model's name invited a derivation it never performed.

## Decision

Adopt LIOS v3.0:

1. **Sleeves A/B/C/D/Z** with one denominator, NLV (L1, L19).
2. **Three money operations only** — DCA waterfall, rebalance band, discretionary
   channel (L20).
3. **Bull–Bear Index 0–100** as a gauge that shifts the A/B/C split inside a
   fixed risky budget and never changes total risky exposure (L21).
4. **Sleeve B sells only on thesis break** — LOS < 60 (L22).
5. **US-situs estate-tax ceiling** tracked as a hard constraint (L23).
6. **Kelly Model v2.0 (L4) and Strategic Reserve (L6) repealed**, numbers
   preserved and gapped.
7. **Glidepath** by age band with a drift-accepted default policy.

## Rationale

- The **gap** is the only variable that is both observable and causally linked to
  what the DCA must do. Making it the controller removes the need for a score in
  the sizing path.
- Removing the score from sizing does **not** remove the forecast problem — it
  stops the forecast from being disguised as a solved optimisation. Targets
  remain policy, and are now labelled policy.
- A **duration sleeve** is the only asset in the architecture that reliably
  appreciates in a growth shock. Cash in bills does not; it is a cash
  substitute, and mislabelling it as a hedge makes the hole invisible.
- A **thesis-exit rule** is the minimum needed for a "rarely sell" policy to be
  safe. Without it, a permanently impaired company stays forever.
- The **rebalance band replaces the trigger ladder**: a drawdown makes the
  defensive block overweight automatically, and the band sells it into the
  underweight risky sleeves. Dry powder becomes a byproduct of drift rather than
  a bucket.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- Gaps are closable, and can be shown to close by arithmetic.
- Removing the score from sizing removes a false-precision controller.
- One denominator eliminates a whole class of reporting errors.
- The portfolio acquires its first genuine diversifier.
- Fewer moving parts: three rules instead of a score, a reserve, a trigger
  ladder and three cash pools.

**Negative**
- Breaking change; every v2.x prompt and template is superseded.
- The risk of "the index is a market call in disguise" is removed by L21, but
  the index still reads like a market call. Naming and reporting matter.
- Drift-accepted glidepath leaves equity exposure high at retirement; requires
  explicit written acknowledgement at every Health Check.
- Targets are now visibly policy choices rather than model outputs. This is
  intellectually honest and psychologically harder.
