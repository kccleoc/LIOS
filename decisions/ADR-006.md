# ADR-006 — Adopt Eight Portfolio Rules in Place of the Locked Decision Layer

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Supersedes:** the L1–L23 constitution established across v1.x–v3.0
**Proposal:** RFC-004

---

## Context

Five version generations added rules and removed almost none. By v3.0 the
constitution carried twenty live entries. Most could not be violated in any
meaningful sense — "reports state dollars" is a style guide, not a governance
constraint. The rules that could be broken were buried among them, and two
independent external reviews plus an internal audit found seven mechanical
defects in the deployment layer that the numbering made hard to see.

Three of those defects were self-inflicted in the v3.0 rewrite: a band that did
not scale across sleeves, a directional restriction written more narrowly than
intended, and a precedence order that omitted the caps entirely.

## Decision

Replace the constitution with eight rules, P1–P8. Move method to a separate
document that can be edited with a changelog entry. Reduce the deployment engine
to two named operations. Delete every discretionary channel.

Specifically:

1. **P1–P8** become the only constitutional content.
2. **`constitution/method.md`** absorbs LOS/LFS, bands, Replacement Index,
   report structure, communication style and glossary pointers.
3. **Top-up and Rebalance** replace "Rule 1/2/3". The numbering collision with
   L2 disappears.
4. **Rule 3 is deleted.** Its function — opportunistic deployment — is already
   performed by Rebalance buying underweight sleeves in a drawdown.
5. **The regime index becomes a dashboard.** It reports; it does not act.
6. **Per-sleeve bands replace the single 8pp band.**
7. **Caps are ranked above rules, and a breach is itself a trigger.**
8. **The transition mandate must have a sunset date and a fallback.**

## Rationale

- A rule belongs in the constitution only if violating it damages the portfolio.
  Twelve of twenty met no such test.
- Two numbering systems in one document guarantee confusion. Removing the
  numbers from the operations removes the ambiguity permanently.
- Every deleted rule was deleted because a *different* rule already performs its
  function. Rule 3 duplicated Rebalance. L19 duplicated P1. L21 constrained an
  index that no longer acts.
- The seven defects were found by review, not by use. That is a signal that the
  rule set had grown past the point where a single person could hold it.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- Eight rules can be remembered. Compliance can be checked by reading one page.
- Method can evolve without a version bump.
- Three failure modes are removed rather than patched.
- The authority behind each rule is now explicit: caps above rules, rules above
  preference.

**Negative**
- Breaking change across the documentation set.
- The regime index is now inert. Its value is diagnostic only, and that may feel
  like a downgrade to anyone who used it to justify action.
- Deleting Rule 3 removes a channel some investors want. It was removed because
  a discretionary channel is indistinguishable from an override in practice.

**Neutral**
- The portfolio itself does not change. Targets, sleeves and the migration plan
  are unaffected.

## Compliance

- `constitution/constitution.md` — P1–P8
- `constitution/method.md` — everything non-constitutional
- `core/deployment.md` — two operations, bands, precedence, transition
- `core/sleeves.md` — sleeve definitions, trim selection, constraints
- `core/regime-index.md` — dashboard only
- `core/glidepath.md` — contribution tilt, age-step grace
- `lios/v3.0.md` — paste-ready prompt rebuilt against the eight rules

## Review

If, over a 12-month period, two or more manual deviations from a Top-up or
Rebalance output occur, the rule set is too complex for the operator regardless of
its correctness. At that point the fallback is a two-fund portfolio, not another
redesign.
