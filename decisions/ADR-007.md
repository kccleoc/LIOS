# ADR-007 — Adopt v5: Four Sleeves with ETF-Basket Conviction

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Supersedes:** ADR-006 (P1–P8 constitution, five sleeves)
**Proposal:** RFC-005

---

## Context

v4.1 defines five sleeves and assigns individual equities to Sleeve B. That
structure has one failure mode it cannot answer: a single company event in a
single-name sleeve is a portfolio event. The operator experienced exactly that —
a chief executive departed, a probe opened, and the position fell roughly thirty
percent while the sector itself did not.

The v4.1 rule set responded the only way it could: define an exit test, run a
review, and sell. That is a rule for after the damage. The operator asked for a
structure that makes the damage smaller in the first place, without changing
total risk, contribution rate, or age-based glidepath.

## Decision

Adopt v5:

1. **A / B / C / Z.** Sleeve D is deleted. Its duration role is taken by C.
2. **A holds ETFs, broad-index and thematic.** This is where a sector view lives.
3. **B is cap-only.** No target, no Top-up, no automatic growth. Additions are an
   Investment Committee decision funded from A.
4. **C is duration.** Rotation and winner-weighting are deleted with the old C.
5. **Risky weight is A + B.** The defensive block is C + Z.
6. **Look-through caps.** Concentration is measured on the underlying company and
   sector, through funds, not on the wrapper.
7. **Soft limits rebalance quarterly within a tolerance band; hard caps fire
   immediately.** A soft ceiling that enforces on a schedule is still a ceiling —
   the schedule is written down, which is the difference between a rule and a
   habit.
8. **Health Check is 15 sections.**

## Rationale

- A rule belongs in the constitution only if breaking it damages the portfolio.
  "B holds individual equities" was a preference wearing a constitutional costume.
- The operator's actual risk appetite did not change. Only the instrument used to
  express it did. A restructure that holds NLV and risky weight constant is a
  cheaper decision than one that moves them.
- Cap-only B removes a contradiction: a sleeve called "satellite" that receives a
  monthly contribution is not a satellite, it is a second core.
- Deferring soft-limit rebalance to a quarterly review reduces churn. The
  tolerance band exists because a rebalance cannot strike targets exactly, and a
  rule that demands an exact strike will be ignored.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- Single-company risk becomes a weight inside a fund rather than a position.
- The operator is not required to have a view on a management team.
- Fewer rules, fewer sleeves, fewer review sections.

**Negative**
- Sector concentration risk is retained and explicitly accepted. A basket does
  not protect against a sector bear market.
- Look-through monitoring depends on fund holdings data and is more work than
  position-level monitoring.
- A soft ceiling can be exceeded between quarterly reviews by design.

**Neutral**
- Total risky weight, glidepath bands and the monthly contribution are unchanged.

## Review

The v4.1 review clause applies unchanged: if two or more manual deviations from a
Top-up or Rebalance output occur within twelve months, the rule set is too complex
for the operator regardless of its correctness. The fallback remains a two-fund
portfolio, not another redesign.

A second test is added for v5 specifically. **If the thematic ETF in Sleeve A is
replaced more than once in twelve months on a view about the sector rather than a
breach of a written rule, then the sleeve has become a trading book and the ETF
structure has failed.** The response is to reduce the thematic allocation to a
fixed, small share and stop reviewing it.
