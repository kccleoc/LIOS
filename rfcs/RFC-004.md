# RFC-004 — Collapse the Constitution from 20 Rules to 8

**Status:** Accepted
**Author:** Portfolio owner
**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Decision:** ADR-006

---

## Summary

Replace the L1–L23 "Locked Decisions" layer with eight Portfolio Rules (P1–P8),
move method documentation out of the constitution, reduce the deployment engine
to two named operations, and remove every discretionary channel.

---

## Problem

Twenty live rules had accumulated across five versions. Three findings drove
this:

**1. Most of them were not rules.** L9 (write dollars), L10 (16 report sections),
L11–L18 (how to score research) are method. Treating them as constitutional meant
every scoring tweak required a version bump, and it hid the eight rules that
actually matter inside twelve that cannot be broken.

**2. The numbering collided.** "Rule 2" (a deployment operation) sat next to
"L2" (Sleeve A's target). Two numbering systems in one document is unreadable.

**3. The deployment layer had grown defects under review.** Two independent
external reviews, and an internal audit, converged on the same failures:

| Defect | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Rule 3 could raise total risky weight above the age-band cap | A "discretionary" 3% per quarter is ~12%/yr of forbidden de-risking in reverse |
| Directional Rule 2 was worded as "only defensive-to-risky" | Banned B→A and Z→D, making Rebalance inert in several states |
| One absolute 8pp band applied to sleeves of 4% and 70% | C's band topped at 6% while its ceiling was 7% — the ceiling was dead code |
| Caps were outside the precedence order | The 10% single-name cap had no enforcement path at all |
| L21 was a one-way gate | It blocked raising risky but never forced it down, and was breached on the first age step |
| The transition had no sunset fallback | If migration criteria were missed, Rebalance would fire on four sleeves at once — the bulk liquidation staging existed to avoid |
| The regime index shifted target weights | Changing targets caused Rebalance to sell names down, contradicting "never acts by selling" |

---

## Proposal

### Eight rules

P1 five sleeves, one denominator · P2 targets from the glidepath, risky weight by
age · P3 two operations · P4 direction limits · P5 Sleeve B exits only on thesis
break, via a 30-day review · P6 caps outrank rules · P7 cap breach is a Rebalance
trigger · P8 reporting.

### Two operations

**Top-up** (monthly, gap-proportional, never sells, with a fallback that routes
to D/Z when risky weight is at the cap) and **Rebalance** (on breach, sells,
direction-limited).

### Deletions

L19 (merged into P1), L21 (nothing left to constrain), Rule 3 (discretionary
channel), the single 8pp band, and the regime index's mechanical effect.

---

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| Keep 23 numbers, add cross-references | The list stays unreadable; the numbering collision persists |
| Merge Sleeves B and C into one Active sleeve | Saves one line of configuration but forces 4–6 exits to fit a name-count limit, which collides with P5. Worse trade |
| Keep the regime index shifting targets | Unbacktested, and it reduces portfolio beta by ~0.025 through composition — market timing in disguise |
| Keep Rule 3 | Both external reviews called it a rationalisation channel; it is worth ~1.2k/yr at best and becomes unusable once risky weight drifts above the cap |

## Consequences

**Positive:** eight rules can be held in memory. Method changes need no ceremony.
Every rule that survives has a failure mode you can name. Three defects are
deleted rather than patched.

**Negative:** breaking change to every document that cited an L-number. The
migration must rewrite `lios/v3.0.md`, the components, and every command that
referenced "Rule 1/2/3".

**Neutral:** the portfolio itself does not change. Targets, sleeves and the
migration plan are unaffected.

---

## Impact

Framework release 4.1.0. Constitution, deployment, sleeves, glidepath and the
paste-ready prompt rewritten. Method content moved to `constitution/method.md`.
