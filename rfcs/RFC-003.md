# RFC-003 — LIOS v3.0: Five Sleeves, Gap Deployment, Bull–Bear Index

**Status:** Accepted
**Author:** Portfolio owner
**Date:** 2026-11-14
**Supersedes:** RFC-002 (which established the v3.0 OSS bifurcation)
**Decision:** ADR-005

---

## Summary

Rebuild the capital architecture and the deployment engine. Replace the
`ABC` denominator with NLV, replace the Kelly Model with a gauge-only
Bull–Bear Index, repeal the Strategic Reserve, and rename the sleeves to
A/B/C/D/Z with cash always last.

---

## Problem

Four defects, in order of severity.

### 1. The Kelly Factor is not Kelly

The model produced a 0–1 "Kelly Factor" from a 0–100 conditions score and
multiplied the monthly DCA by it. It computed no probability, no payoff ratio,
and no bankroll. Kelly's `f* = p − q/b` and its continuous form `(μ − r)/σ²`
were never evaluated.

Worse, the object being sized was a **flow**, not a **position**. A Kelly
fraction applies to wealth. A factor of 0.25 on a book of roughly 200,000 with
a monthly contribution of 1,500 deploys 375 — about 1/139th of what a genuine
0.25 fraction of the same book implies. The name implied a derivation that never
happened, which made a risk-appetite dial look like an optimisation.

### 2. Two denominators

Sleeve A and Sleeve C were measured against `ABC = NLV − D − SR`; Sleeve B caps
against NLV. A dollar therefore carried two weights. The resulting "gap"
widened when it was funded by the same cash arriving into the base, and could
not be closed by any deployment rule.

### 3. The score ignored the distance to target

A book 5pp underweight received the same deployment as a book exactly on target.
The one variable that should drive allocation — the gap — was not an input.

### 4. No duration sleeve

The portfolio held no instrument with duration. Short-dated Treasury ETFs were
treated as bonds. They are cash: they do not rally when equities fall, so the
portfolio had a stated desire for diversification and no asset that supplied it.

---

## Proposal

### Sleeves

```
NLV = A + B + C + D + Z
```

A core index · B conviction (1–4, equal weight) · C satellite (winner-weighted)
· D duration (>3y, hedged) · Z cash equivalents (duration ≈ 0, always last).

### Deployment — three rules only

1. **DCA waterfall** — allocate contributions pro-rata to the gap, with a
   standstill term equal to `w_s × DCA`. Never sells.
2. **Rebalance band** — sell overweight to target when any sleeve breaches the
   band; buy underweight pro-rata. This is the crash response.
3. **Discretionary channel** — ≤1 per quarter, max 3% of NLV, funded from D or Z.

### Bull–Bear Index

Keep the five dimensions and weights. Change the output to 0–100, higher =
more bearish. Change the job: the index shifts the **A/B/C split inside a fixed
risky budget**, implemented by starving sleeves of contributions, never by
selling. One band step per quarter maximum.

### Glidepath

Age bands 45→65+, risky weight 80%→60%, Sleeve B decaying to zero. Default
policy is drift-accepted: targets govern new money only; no de-risking sales.

### Constraints

Single-name effective exposure 10%, factor 25%, duration 15%, risky per age
band, US-situs below the estate-tax exemption, satellite ceiling 7%.

---

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| Keep Kelly Factor, rename it | Leaves the sizing defect; a flow fraction still ignores the gap |
| Compute real Kelly `f* = μ/σ²` | `f*` is linear in an unestimable `μ`; the plausible range moves `f*` from 0% to 125% of wealth. Usable as a diagnostic, not as a monthly controller |
| Keep `ABC` | Cannot be reconciled with Merton-style bankroll thinking; produces unclosable gaps |
| Keep the Strategic Reserve | Negative EV (payoff ratio must exceed ~5.7:1); duplicates the rebalance band |
| Trend overlay instead of rebalance | Higher discipline requirement, whipsaw cost, and conflicts with the investor's stated preference for no exit rule |

## Migration

Staged over approximately 20 trading days, with the single-name trim staged over
up to 60 days on price strength. No sale is made to satisfy a rule; every trade
is funded by a target gap fixed by the migration plan.

## Risks

- **Forecast sensitivity is unchanged.** Removing the score from sizing does not
  make targets more knowable. Targets are policy, and are labelled as such.
- **B and C shrink.** Fewer idiosyncratic bets means lower expected dispersion.
  Accepted: the constraint set is deliberately conservative on single-name risk.
- **Drift-accepted glidepath leaves equity high at retirement.** Accepted and
  documented; the investor does not intend to draw down capital.

---

## Impact

Breaking. `ABC`, Kelly Factor, Kelly Reserve and the Strategic Reserve are
deleted. All v2.x paste-ready prompts stop being authoritative. Every report
template changes.
