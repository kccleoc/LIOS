# RFC-005 — v5: Four Sleeves, ETF-Basket Conviction, Cap-Only Satellites

**Status:** Accepted (implemented as v5.0.0)
**Date:** 2026-11-15
**Supersedes:** RFC-004 / v4.1 (P1–P8)
**ADR:** `decisions/ADR-007.md`

---

## Motivation

v4.1 splits the risky block into three sleeves — A (broad index), B (individual
equities), C (thematic rotation) — and asks the operator to pick individual
companies for B. In practice the operator paid a single-company price for a
sector view: one governance event at one holding cost roughly thirty percent of
that position, and no part of the rule set had an answer that was not "sell the
name."

The operator's requirement is narrower than a new strategy: **express a sector
conviction as a basket, so that no single company's failure can break it, while
keeping the same total risk, the same contribution schedule, and the same
age-based glidepath.**

## Decision

1. **Four sleeves.** A / B / C / Z. D is deleted; its duration role moves to C.
2. **A holds ETFs** — broad-index *and* sector/thematic. "Conviction" means a
   documented, reviewable view expressed through a basket.
3. **B is cap-only.** A ceiling, no target. No Top-up allocation, ever.
   Additions are an Investment Committee decision funded from A.
4. **C is duration.** Bonds beyond three years. The satellite rotation rules that
   lived in old C are deleted, not migrated.
5. **Risky = A + B.** C + Z is the defensive block.
6. **Look-through caps replace position-level caps.** The unit of concentration
   control is the underlying company and the sector, counted through funds.
7. **Quarterly Rebalance with a tolerance band** replaces event-only rebalance
   for soft limits. Hard caps still fire immediately.
8. **Health Check is 15 sections.** The deleted sleeve's review merges into C.

## Consequences

**Positive**
- A single-company failure inside a fund is a weight, not an event.
- The operator is no longer required to manufacture conviction in a name.
- The rule set gets smaller: three money rules become two, plus one tolerance.
- Cap-only B ends the contradiction of a "satellite" sleeve that grows monthly.

**Negative**
- A 25-name sector fund is still a sector bet. This is documented as accepted.
- Look-through accounting is heavier than position-level accounting and depends
  on fund holdings data that can be stale.
- A soft ceiling depends on a scheduled review; between reviews B can exceed it.
- Fees rise marginally: a sector fund's expense ratio replaces direct ownership.

**Neutral**
- Total risky weight, the age glidepath, and the contribution schedule are
  unchanged. This is a repackaging, not a re-risking.
